## 1. Roman Hindi (as drafted in the pilot plan) — 41 words

> Aaj subah, Chintu ki patang aasmaan mein nahi — bijli ke taar par atki thi.
> Neeche gali mein sab has rahe the. Upar chhat par, Chintu soch raha tha.
> Phir usne dor halke se khinchi… aur patang neeche nahi, upar udd gayi!

- Caption chunks (from the plan): [aaj subah] [chintu ki] [patang] [aasmaan mein] [nahi] — [bijli ke] [taar par] [atki thi] / [neeche gali] [mein sab] [has rahe] [the] [upar chhat] [par chintu] [soch raha] [tha] / [phir usne] [dor halke] [se khinchi] [aur patang] [neeche nahi] [upar udd] [gayi]
- Sound-off tag on the kite: **PATANG**

## 2. Devanagari Hindi (same words, same meaning) — 41 words

> आज सुबह, चिंटू की पतंग आसमान में नहीं — बिजली के तार पर अटकी थी।
> नीचे गली में सब हँस रहे थे। ऊपर छत पर, चिंटू सोच रहा था।
> फिर उसने डोर हल्के से खींची… और पतंग नीचे नहीं, ऊपर उड़ गई!

- Sound-off tag on the kite: **पतंग**
- Note: same shots, timings and SFX cues as the Roman version — only the text changes.

## 3. English (trimmed to the same 41 words, so it still fits ~15 s) — 41 words

> Down in the lane, everyone laughed. Up on the roof, Chintu thought.
> Then he tugged the string gently… and the kite didn't fall — it flew up!

- Caption chunks: [this morning] [chintu's kite] [wasn't in] [the sky] — [stuck on] [a power] [line] / [down in] [the lane] [everyone laughed] [up on] [the roof] [chintu thought] / [then he] [tugged the] [string gently] [and the kite] [didn't fall] [it flew] [up]
- Sound-off tag on the kite: **KITE**
- Note: a literal translation runs to 45 words (~18 s at 2.5 words/sec), so this version tightens "was laughing / was thinking" to "laughed / thought" to keep the 15-second shape. The joke and the payoff line are unchanged.
